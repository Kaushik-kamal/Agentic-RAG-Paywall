# Data Protection and Regulatory Compliance

## Lawful basis for processing

Processing personal data requires a lawful basis identified *before* processing
begins. The six bases are consent, contract, legal obligation, vital interests,
public task, and legitimate interests. You cannot switch basis afterwards to
rescue processing that has already occurred.

Consent must be freely given, specific, informed and unambiguous, given by a clear
affirmative act. Pre-ticked boxes and silence do not qualify. Consent is not
freely given where there is a clear imbalance of power, which is why employers
rarely rely on it for employee data.

Legitimate interests requires a documented three-part assessment: identify the
interest, show the processing is necessary for it, and balance it against the
rights of the data subject.

## Data subject rights

Subjects have rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability,
and objection, plus rights concerning automated decision-making. Requests must
normally be answered within one month, extendable by two further months for complex
requests, and free of charge unless manifestly unfounded or excessive.

The right to erasure is not absolute. It does not apply where processing is
necessary for compliance with a legal obligation, for the establishment or defence
of legal claims, or for archiving in the public interest.

## Breach notification

A personal data breach must be reported to the supervisory authority within
**72 hours** of becoming aware of it, unless it is unlikely to result in a risk to
individuals. Where the risk is *high*, affected individuals must also be informed
without undue delay. Encryption that renders the data unintelligible can remove the
obligation to notify individuals.

The clock starts at awareness of the breach, not at completion of the
investigation. A partial notification followed by detail is expected, and delay
pending certainty is a common enforcement finding.

## Accountability and transfers

Controllers must demonstrate compliance, not merely achieve it: records of
processing activities, data protection impact assessments for high-risk processing,
privacy by design and by default, and appointment of a data protection officer
where processing is large-scale or systematic.

International transfers require an adequacy decision, appropriate safeguards such as
standard contractual clauses combined with a transfer impact assessment, or a
narrow derogation. Reliance on standard clauses alone is insufficient where the
destination country's surveillance law undermines them in practice.

## Retention

Data may be kept no longer than necessary for the purpose. "Indefinitely, in case
it is useful" is not a retention period. A defensible schedule states a duration per
category, a trigger for the clock, and a deletion mechanism that actually runs.
